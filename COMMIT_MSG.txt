refactor(vouchers): delete the dead duplicate voucher surfaces, and the mis-named QR field with them

Closes planning #273 and #272 together, because one deletion resolves both.

AdminFuelVoucherController (api/admin/fuel-vouchers) duplicated
AdminVoucherController (api/admin/vouchers): three of its four handlers were the
same shared instances, and no client called any of its routes. Keeping a second
route onto a money path is what makes "which one is real" unanswerable later -
AdminVoucherController has since grown bulk-action, unblock, unarchive and
verification, and a caller who found the fuel-vouchers twin would reach for
those and get a 404.

The duplicate was also the weaker copy of the two, which settles any "keep it
just in case":

- GetFuelVouchersQueryHandler projects raw FuelVoucher entities rather than a
  DTO, so every column of the table is published and adding one auto-publishes
  it. That is the failure AnonymousEndpointSurfaceTests exists to prevent on the
  public side, and the admin list handler GetAdminVouchersQueryHandler does
  project an explicit DTO.
- GetQrCodesQueryHandler has no paging at all: it materialises every voucher
  with ToListAsync and projects in memory. Its sibling comment in
  GetFuelVouchersQueryHandler explains exactly why that is a problem, and the
  QR handler then does the thing the comment warns about.

That closes #272 as well. The qrCodeUrl ambiguity was between two producers:
GetQrCodesQueryHandler put v.QrPayload into a field named QrCodeUrl, while
Import/GetVouchersQuery documents the same name as an addressable URL and its
test pins it as one. With the QR handler gone, the only remaining QrCodeUrl is
the import one, and the name means one thing again. Nothing needed renaming,
because nothing reads the mis-named value.

The mobile type still declares qrCodeUrl and copies it through from
/api/sync/orders, where the backend sends QrPayload and QrCodeData - never
qrCodeUrl - so that field has always been undefined. Left alone here: it is a
client-side type, and touching it would widen this change beyond the deletion
that justified it.

Tests for the two deleted handlers went with them. Nothing else referenced
GetFuelVouchersQueryHandler, GetQrCodesQueryHandler, or their DI registrations.
README no longer lists the removed routes.
