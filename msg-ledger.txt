feat(ledger): expired stock is written off in the books, not only in the P&L

The nightly expiry sweep retired a voucher to `Expired` and recorded the loss in the
per-batch P&L — and the ledger carried nothing. Account 281 (Товари на складі) still
held the voucher's cost, so the one book an accountant reads said the stock was there
while the stock had been written off. Two books, two answers, and the divergence was
invisible until somebody read both.

**The write-off is Dr 807 (Витрати від утрати товарів) / Cr 281 (Товари на складі).**

807 rather than 808 (Витрати товарів, the cost of goods sold) because 808 is what a
SALE books, and a voucher nobody bought is not a sale. 807 is the element the
Ukrainian Plan of Accounts (MinFin Order No. 291) gives for lost goods, and it rolls up
under 80 with the cost of sales while staying readable on its own — because "how much
did we lose to expired stock" is a question the account has to be able to answer without
a report. It is a subaccount of 80, so no new top-level account is invented, and the
chart's own rule still holds: only the accounts this business uses are seeded.

**Who stands behind a posting made by a machine.** The journal will not take a posted
entry that names nobody — "a posted entry without both a moment and a person is an
entry nobody can be asked about" — and a nightly sweep has nobody of its own. The
operator who turned the sweep on does: the person who decided that lapsed unsold stock
is written off is the one the entry is about, and the admin screen already records who
changed a setting. So the write-off is posted under the operator, with no invented user
and no schema change.

**And when the setting names nobody, nothing is booked at all.** A voucher retired
without its entry is the exact divergence this change removes, and attributing the entry
to a made-up user would be a worse lie. So the job refuses, logs an error, and says what
to do.

**One flag, as before.** `ExpiredVoucherLoss:Enabled` gates the status flip and the
ledger posting together — two flags for one business fact is how the two books drift
apart. The dry run still says what it *would* post: the entry count, the accounts, and
the amount.

**The posting happens BEFORE the status flip**, and that order is the safety. If the
posting fails, the voucher is not retired, so the next night's run tries again — and
the posting is idempotent on `(source_event, source_type, source_id)` with the voucher
as the id, so the retry reuses the entry instead of writing a second one.

**One entry per voucher**, because the idempotency key is the voucher. A group-level
entry would be fewer rows and would skip a partly-processed group on a re-run, which is
how a write-off goes half-booked.
